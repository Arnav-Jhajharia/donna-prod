what this is

a stake in the ground

these aren't screens to build. they're seven arguments about what the mobile app is for. each flow is a posture donna can take. read them, find the ones that feel wrong, push back on those. that's the whole point of this document.

every page below follows the locked design system from the dashboard project. paper canvas. one rust per screen. serif upright for the editorial voice, sans for utility. lowercase everywhere. hairlines, not boxes. if a page violates a rule, the rule wins.

paper first

warm off-white is the canvas. black is a value, never the default.

one rust per screen

the brand accent is for the one thing that matters most. never decoration.

italic is emphasis

upright serif carries the editorial tone. italic is a word, a phrase, the wordmark.

signals are states

moss, amber, oxblood appear only when something is true about the world.

hairlines not boxes

we contain with 1px rules and whitespace, not filled containers.

quiet and specific

"saved two minutes ago." not "autosaved successfully." no exclamations. ever.

04 · first exchange. three bursts. donna acknowledges, sets expectations, invites. no buttons to "skip" — the keyboard is the answer.

things to argue

  • the letter feels like marketing. alternative: skip the letter, drop the user straight into the chat. the relationship starts faster but the voice doesn't land. test both.
  • three fields is still a form. we could ask all of this conversationally in flow 04 instead. costs one screen but feels less corporate.
  • the splash is inverse. if dark mode is a user preference, the inverse splash will feel disconnected from the rest. consider a paper splash with a small inverse "d" monogram.
  • "begin" vs "continue" vs nothing. donna doesn't really use cta verbs in her voice. could be a tap-anywhere splash. trade-off: less discoverable.

07 · held back. donna's reasoning surfaced. pills are state, not category. she shows the one she did act on (gas bill) for legibility, in oxblood — earned, never decorative.

things to argue

  • "three things" is a strong claim. what if there's only one? two? does she pad? alternative: variable count, but the headline copy changes ("one thing", "five things"). more honest, less consistent.
  • cards vs. inline narrative. a chief of staff would talk, not give you cards. version b is the home screen as a single morning paragraph from donna, in serif. cards are skimmable; paragraphs are intimate.
  • does the bottom nav belong? tabs are explicitly retired in the dashboard system (SYSTEM.md §10). a drawer is the spec'd pattern. but mobile thumb reach makes tabs hard to beat. this is the biggest design rule i'm bending.
  • "things i held back" is novel. nobody does this. it could feel surveilling. or it could be the most trust-building screen in the whole app. needs user testing.

10 · long-press. verbs in donna's vocabulary: remember, forget, follow up. no icons. a tiny helper line under each — she's narrating what the action does. the bubble behind has a rust outline showing what's selected.

things to argue

  • day dividers in the middle of a thread. imessage does them, whatsapp doesn't. on a chief-of-staff app, the time matters more than usual. keep, but maybe softer.
  • inline pill replies vs a "buttons" row. pills feel like donna's voice continuing. buttons feel like a form. but pills are a non-standard input pattern and may need a hint to be discoverable.
  • three verbs on long-press. "follow up" overlaps with "remind me" which is a phrase the user might type. is the long-press verb redundant or essential?
  • the user bubble is ink-900. on dark mode, both bubbles converge in color and tonality. needs an inverse-mode chat treatment that's not just role swaps.

13 · forget. the only oxblood button in the entire app. dialog uses serif h3 because forgetting deserves the editorial register. "can't be undone" is a caption, not a banner — quiet, specific.

things to argue

  • memory editing. if you can edit a memory, it stops being something donna saw and becomes something you wrote. consider read-only memories + "forget"-only — closer to the real model.
  • "how i use this" is a strong assertion. donna probably doesn't know exactly how she uses a memory until the moment. honest version says "i factor this into recommendations." vaguer, less reassuring.
  • quoting the original message verbatim. intimate, but also makes the memory feel like surveillance. alternative: paraphrase. less true, less creepy.
  • forget is irreversible. from a product perspective, undoable forgets are a footgun (people forget by accident). from an emotional perspective, irreversibility is the point — it's a ceremony.

16 · configure. moss dot inline in the eyebrow signals state. "follow-up posture" is donna's word for it — quiet vs loud is more honest than "frequency". disconnect is a ghost button, low affordance — destructive without being dramatic.

things to argue

  • moss/amber/muted dots inline in row titles. reads at a glance but adds visual noise. alternative: dots only in the right-side pill. cleaner, harder to scan.
  • "read only" vs "read and draft" as the two modes. what about "read and send"? probably a step too far for v1. but the absence is a position.
  • exclusions are user-typed strings. a real product needs a picker. but the picker becomes a screen of its own, and donna isn't a control panel.
  • disconnect is one tap with no confirm. intentional — it's reversible (reconnect any time). but if a connection took 20 minutes of oauth to set up, the user will feel the cost.

things to argue

  • "i read 42. three want you." a strong claim. if donna picks wrong even 10% of the time, this becomes the worst feature. needs a soft fallback.

  • serif email body. deliberate departure from sans chat bubbles — emails are letters, chats are messages. but it means the same content type renders differently in different surfaces.

20 · quiet hours. the times are serif 36px — a deliberate flex, because time is editorial here. day pills are circles with single letters, accent for selected, surface for unselected. weekend is off by default — donna assumes you sleep in.

things to argue

  • only four settings sections. push for a fifth: "what to remember by default". or push to remove "voice tone" — letting users tune donna's voice contradicts the locked product voice.
  • "except urgent" is undefined. donna decides. that's load-bearing trust, and a single bad call breaks the feature.
  • serif 36px time numerals. beautiful, possibly precious. sans 600 tabular nums at 32px would be more legible and more donna's actual product voice.
  • "delete all my data" sits in settings. some products bury this two screens deep. donna's voice would put it right in the open. but the open placement makes accidental taps more likely.

the seven biggest decisions

if you only argue these

  1. a letter, or skip the letter? flow 01 stakes the editorial voice on the second screen. removing it costs the voice but speeds the relationship.
  2. three things, or however many things? flow 02 hard-codes a count. variable is more honest but harder to design around.
  3. a bottom tab bar, or a single home with a drawer? tabs are easier on the thumb but explicitly retired in SYSTEM.md §10. drawer is spec-correct, slower to navigate.
  4. memory editable, or only forgettable? editing turns donna's view of you into something you author. forgetting alone is closer to how memory works.
  5. a separate inbox copilot, or chat with attachments? flow 06 is a dedicated surface. an alternative is everything goes through chat — emails arrive as bubbles donna comments on. one feature, one fewer page.
  6. donna's voice configurable? the locked product voice in CLAUDE.md says no. but the settings screen exposes a "tone" item. one of them is wrong.
  7. "i held this back from you" — does it ship? flow 02 page 07. either the most trust-building screen or the most surveilling. nothing in between.
